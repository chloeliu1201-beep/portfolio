02 — Textile
Textile / CMF Design Biomaterial2026

Touch the Light

RCA MA Textiles graduation project

Project overview

Touch the Light is a flexible, translucent biomaterial I developed in 2026 for my MA Textiles graduation project at the Royal College of Art. It brings touch, colour and light together in everyday surfaces and explores a possible alternative to leather finishes.

At the RCA, I began by exploring biomaterials for textile repair, testing coatings on fabric and casting standalone sheets. The focus shifted to how the sheets felt and transmitted light. I adjusted their thickness and forming conditions, chose blade cutting for cleaner edges, and assembled some of the cut pieces using interlocking structures.

The finished sheets and modular swatches have a frosted finish and feel flexible and cool to the touch, with few visible fingerprints after handling. Colour sits within the sheets, and overlapping layers produce new tones in transmitted light.

Potential applications · Home décor, automotive interiors, fashion and accessories, lighting and spatial installations.

Behind the scenes

Exhibition workshop

At the RCA graduate exhibition, visitors, including children, handled the material and made their own pieces. Its bright colours and ease of cutting made the offcuts from my samples easy to reuse. Visitors cut and combined the scraps into small accessories they could take home and use.

Textile / Biomaterial2024

Revived of Shadowplay

Shadow puppetry and biomaterial · 2024

Project overview

After visiting the Shaanxi Ming–Qing Shadow Play Museum in October 2023, I began developing thin, translucent biomaterial sheets as an alternative to the cattle hide traditionally used in shadow puppetry.

Drawing on puppet silhouettes and the opera-inspired characters in The Legend of Sealed Book, I developed shapes and colour palettes, laser-cut the motifs, and tested different ways of joining them. The pieces explore how traditional shadow-play patterns change when worn, held up to light, or used in a lantern.

Exhibition

In March 2026, during Puyuan Haute Couture Fashion Week, the project was selected for the intangible heritage and sustainable design section of Global Impact, a UK–China art and design exhibition co-organised by Donghua University and the Royal College of Art.

Textile / 3D Knit & Crochet2024

Deciphering Humanity

Knitted mixed materials · 2024

Project overview

Reading Nineteen Eighty-Four and Brave New World, I began thinking about how technology shapes behaviour through surveillance or comfort. When the systems around us start to guide choices that feel like our own, what happens to free will and personal identity?

I translated that question into knitted forms that recall parts of the human body. I combined mixed fibres with acrylic, optical fibre and PVC. Soft loops sit inside rigid transparent panels, and glowing lines trace the knit structures, making them look both bodily and technological.

3D crochet at the RCA

At the Royal College of Art, I extended the project through three-dimensional crochet. I combined flexible tubing with raised loops and open structures, then tested the samples around the hand to see how they bent, shifted and changed shape with movement.

03 — Fashion
Fashion / Textile printing
B / Undergraduate graduation · textile printing

Kaleidoscope

Clothing is one way women express different facets of their identity across everyday settings. Kaleidoscope, my undergraduate womenswear collection, uses textile print to express how those facets can coexist.

Drawing on Cubist approaches to image-making, I used Photoshop and digital printing to translate layered imagery into fabric. Folds, layers and volume shift how the prints appear across each look.

Fashion / Unisex Collection2023

Freedom to…

2023 / Donghua University × Burberry

Project overview

Freedom to… is a unisex collection developed in 2023 through a Donghua University collaboration with Burberry. Designed around walking in Shanghai and London, it considers changing weather, ease of movement and individual expression.

The palette draws on city architecture and everyday dress. Five core fabrics were compared for hand feel, weight and intended use, including two waterproof options. Adjustable and deconstructed elements let the coat change shape in motion. The project resulted in a finished trench coat and designs for a bag, footwear and eyewear.
